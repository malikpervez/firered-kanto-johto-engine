## The journey

The portal opens while the group is hanging out at Brian's place. Brian and Nick are playing 2K; Tim has travel tabs open; everyone else is offering advice nobody requested. Giratina drags all seven through a distorted reflection in the television. The game shows their arrival in Kanto; the living-room origin is established through their arguments, avoiding a compulsory new opening map.

At first they want Wi-Fi, somewhere to sleep and a refund. They assume the local rules are nonsense. Their Pokémon gradually make those rules matter: resting, feeding a partner, learning an actual move and sticking around when somebody is frightened.

Their shared goal is getting home together. Strange reflections occasionally reveal Brian's living room. The group eventually discovers that the crossing opens only when Giratina permits it, and they insist on taking their willing Pokémon with them. This is an original story convention, not a new claim about canonical portal mechanics. There is no collectible portal key, legendary capture requirement or hunt for seven fragments.

The main trainer continues the original campaign. The friends handle their own smaller problems, win local practice matches and help people between major events. They never clear the player's Rocket headquarters, acquire the player's badges, resolve Shadow Lugia or become offscreen Champions.

### Conversation and reference rules

Give a scene a concrete concern, a sustained exchange about it, a small action or realization, and a natural way back to the journey. Let friends disagree, answer the actual objection, get distracted and return to it. They do not take turns delivering one unrelated punchline each. Quiet scenes can be shorter but should still have a human exchange. Player choices change a few conversational turns, not just the final quip.

LB's model is the owner's example: someone says it is all the same; he answers, **"First of all, NOTHING WAS THE SAME."** The punchline depends on the preceding words and his unnecessary seriousness about the album title. Other friends can argue about his timing, occasionally recognize the reference, or bring him back to the task. Do not constantly interrupt him before he delivers the reference.

The titles used here are **Nothing Was the Same**, **Take Care**, **Thank Me Later** and **Views**. They are album references, not invented Drake quotations or excerpts from lyrics. Title spelling is supported by the [label catalog](https://www.universal-music.de/drake/musik), and the official pages for [Take Care](https://drakerelated.com/collections/take-care), [Thank Me Later](https://checkout.drakerelated.com/collections/thank-me-later) and [Views](https://drakerelated.com/collections/views). All surrounding dialogue is original. Keep contextual references spaced out; LB also talks normally, cares for his Pokémon and participates in the group's decisions.

## Integration and persistence

All friend scenes and battles are optional side content. Native movement, services, rewards, Gym challenges, rival scenes, League access, regional travel and legendary quests remain available without completing this arc. The friends never require a player payment, donated Pokémon or mandatory battle win.

The narrative order is arrival → partners → home reflection → Johto growth → farewell → visit. Use a reviewed group story stage with any genuinely necessary independent battle receipts; do not allocate a byte per joke or reuse unrelated flags. Separate the proposed FG/B labels from actual engine IDs. Keep Kanto state through the Johto initializer, preserve existing saves, and audit the current registry/capacity before binding.

The group normally occupies one current camp or encounter at a time. Retire earlier placements once their journey advances; do not populate every town with seven simultaneous copies. Show two or three foreground speakers where map resources are tight, with the others implied nearby. Actual appearances of all seven at arrival/farewell require measured actor, palette and scene budgets, not invisible or unrelated stand-ins.

For a player who skips an optional meeting, a later scene can explain the friends' offscreen progress without claiming the player witnessed it: "We landed near Viridian. We've been trying to get home ever since." Preserve the event's prerequisites for the group's own story. Do not force a late-game player to replay early trainer teams across Kanto. Supported late adoption needs an explicit entry stage, honest introductory dialogue and battle availability; never mark missed battles as won or their rewards claimed.

Giratina is a scripted presence only: no battle, gift, egg, trade, party entry, Pokédex grant or obtainable reward. An implementation must audit other active acquisition routes before claiming game-wide nonobtainability. The friends' later visit does not expose a reusable portal system or an explorable Distortion World.

Tim's adult jokes remain brief and human-directed. Ham's vulgar interjections are not sexual actions or anatomy jokes about Pokémon. Jon's anger is expressed through words; no attacks on children, cruelty to Pokémon or group-directed hate are part of his voice. LB and Xavier have the specific familiar-language allowance recorded above; it does not extend to Jon or other characters. The player remains their own age/identity and never becomes a participant in adult sexual activity. LB can love Drake without importing copyrighted lyrics or pretending invented lines are real quotes.

Sprites, portrait art, portal/home-view assets, sound cues and exact trainer presentation remain design dependencies. Reuse only verified approved assets that actually convey the scene. Missing resources must be reported; a text-only substitute is not proof that a requested visual moment works.
